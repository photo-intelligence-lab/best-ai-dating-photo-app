## Why dating photos are a different problem from headshots

A LinkedIn headshot has one job: look competent and approachable against a plain background. A dating profile needs variety. Hinge shows six photos. Tinder users flick through several before deciding. A profile made of six near-identical studio portraits looks like a fake account, or like someone who only owns one shirt.

So the questions for a dating photo app are different:

- **Can it make photos that look like real life?** Coffee shops, hikes, a dinner, a dog, a beach. Not just backdrops.

- **Does it keep your face consistent and accurate?** The person who shows up to the date should be the person in the photos.

- **How many photos do you get?** You'll throw away a lot. Volume gives you room to be picky.

- **Is it a one-off cost or a monthly bill?** Most people need a profile refresh, not a content machine.

## What makes an AI dating photo actually work

Regardless of app, the photos that perform tend to share a few traits:

**A clear, well-lit face as photo one.** No sunglasses, no hat shadow, no group. People decide fast.

**At least one full-body or three-quarter shot.** Leaving it out makes people wonder why.

**Context that says something.** A photo at a climbing wall, with a guitar, at a market. It gives someone an easy opener.

**Variety in setting and outfit.** Indoors and outdoors, day and evening, casual and slightly dressed-up.

**Accuracy.** This is the big one. AI can make you look like a slightly better-lit version of yourself; it shouldn't make you look like someone else. If a result changes your jawline, your hairline or your build, don't use it. A date that starts with "you look different from your photos" doesn't go well, and most apps' terms frown on misleading images.

## How to get the most from your selfies

Upload 15 to 20 recent photos with varied angles, expressions and lighting. Include some with a real smile. Leave out anything filtered, anything with sunglasses and anything more than a year or two old. Then, when the results arrive, choose like a friend would: the ones that look like you on your best day, in places you'd actually go.

## FAQ

### Which AI dating photo app gives the most photos for the money?

Narkis.ai, at $27 for up to 200 photos ($0.135 each). DatePhotos.AI is next among dating-focused apps at $29 for 80.

### Are AI dating photos allowed on Tinder, Hinge and Bumble?

The apps prohibit impersonation and misleading photos. AI-enhanced photos of yourself that accurately show what you look like are a grey area most users navigate by keeping results realistic. If a photo doesn't look like you, don't post it.

### Do AI dating photos really get more matches?

Better photos usually help, and that's true whether they're AI or not. Narkis reports customers going to 25+ matches per week and up to 400% more matches, and offers a matches guarantee on its dating product. Those are customer-reported numbers, and your own results depend on your profile, location and app.

### Is a subscription dating photo app worth it?

Rarely. You need one strong set of photos and the occasional refresh. Narkis, DatePhotos.AI and Aragon are one-time; ROAST and PhotoAI bill on a recurring basis.

### How many photos should a dating profile have?

Hinge requires six. On Tinder and Bumble, four to six good, varied photos usually beats nine mediocre ones.
